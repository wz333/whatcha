Privacy Policy

Effective November 5, 2026

Whatcha is an iPhone app that helps you learn English words for everyday things. This policy explains what information the app uses, where it goes, and what choices you have.

When we say "Whatcha," "we," or "us," we mean the app and its developers.

At a glance

  • No account needed. You can use Whatcha without providing your name, email address, or phone number.
  • Your learning stays yours. Your words, progress, and preferences are stored on your iPhone. We don't collect or access this information.
  • Your voice stays on your iPhone. Speaking exercises use on-device speech recognition. We don't save audio recordings or upload your speech.
  • Ads are provided by Google. If you don't have Whatcha Plus, Google AdMob may collect certain device and usage information to deliver and measure ads.
  • Reports are optional. If you report a problem or email us, we receive only the information you choose to share, along with limited technical details described below.

Information stored on your iPhone

Whatcha saves information locally to make your learning experience work, including:

  • Words you've learned, your answers, and when each word is due for review.
  • Your favorite and removed words.
  • Your study history, daily progress, and streaks.
  • Your preferences, including daily goals, languages, and appearance.
  • Your Whatcha Plus purchase status, so the app knows whether to show ads.

The Quick Review widget also uses a small amount of shared storage on your iPhone to access words due for review and their pictures.

Your learning information stays on your device. We don't collect, receive, or access it.

Your app data may be included in iCloud or computer backups if you have backups enabled. Apple handles these backups according to its Privacy Policy.

You can erase your learning progress using Reset Progress in the Me tab. Deleting Whatcha removes its locally stored app data from your iPhone, although older copies may remain in existing device backups.

Microphone and speech recognition

Some exercises let you practice saying words aloud. These exercises require permission to use your microphone and Apple's speech recognition service.

Whatcha uses on-device speech recognition to turn what you say into text and check your answer. The audio is processed temporarily and is not saved as a recording. Recognition results are discarded after they're used to check your answer.

We don't receive or store your voice, and Whatcha does not send your speech to our servers or Apple's speech recognition servers.

You can disable speaking exercises in the Me tab or revoke microphone and speech recognition permissions in your iPhone's Settings.

Problem reports

If something looks wrong with a word, picture, pronunciation, or exercise, you can send us a report using the question mark on a word.

A report includes:

  • The reason you selected and any note you choose to write.
  • The word, its category, and the exercise you were doing.
  • The app version, iOS version, and time of the report.

Reports don't automatically include your name, email address, location, or a device identifier.

Reports are sent through Google Apps Script to our support inbox in Gmail. Google processes this information as the provider of those services. Like other internet services, Google's servers can receive your IP address when you submit a report. We don't receive or store that IP address ourselves.

If a report can't be sent immediately, it is temporarily stored on your iPhone and retried the next time you open Whatcha.

We use reports to identify and fix problems with the app. Please avoid including personal information in your notes.

Emails to support

If you contact us by email, we'll receive your email address and the information you include in your message.

We use this information to respond to you, resolve issues, and improve Whatcha. You can request deletion of your support correspondence by contacting us.

Advertising

Whatcha uses Google AdMob to display occasional full-screen ads between lessons for users without Whatcha Plus.

Google's advertising software may collect and process information such as:

  • Network information: Your IP address and approximate location derived from it.
  • Device information: Your iPhone model, iOS version, language, and app- or device-related identifiers.
  • Advertising activity: Ads shown to you and how you interact with them, including views and taps.
  • Diagnostic information: Crash reports, performance data, and other technical information.

Google uses this information to deliver and measure ads, improve its advertising services, and help prevent fraud.

Whatcha does not request Apple's App Tracking Transparency permission. This means the app does not receive permission to access your iPhone's advertising identifier (IDFA) for tracking. However, Google may still process other information through its advertising SDK.

Depending on your location, you may be presented with choices about how information is used for advertising. Where required, Whatcha provides a way to review applicable advertising privacy choices.

Google handles advertising data according to its own policies:

  • How Google uses information from apps that use its services
  • Google Privacy Policy

We can view aggregated advertising statistics, such as ad impressions and estimated earnings, but we don't receive your individual advertising profile.

Whatcha Plus removes ads. The app does not request ad placements while an active Whatcha Plus purchase is recognized.

Purchases

Whatcha Plus is purchased through Apple's App Store.

Apple processes your payment information. We don't receive your payment card details, name, or Apple Account credentials.

Whatcha receives purchase entitlement information from Apple to determine whether Whatcha Plus is active and to restore your purchase when needed.

For more information, see the Apple Privacy Policy.

Children

Whatcha is designed for adults and teenagers learning English. It is not directed to children under 13.

We don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has shared personal information with us, please contact us so we can review and delete it as appropriate.

How long we keep information

Your learning progress and preferences remain on your iPhone until you reset or delete the relevant app data.

Problem reports and support correspondence are retained in our support inbox only for as long as reasonably needed to investigate issues, respond to messages, and improve Whatcha, unless a longer period is required by law.

You can ask us to delete information you've submitted. Since problem reports don't contain an account identifier, we may not be able to identify a specific report without additional details.

Information processed by Apple or Google is subject to their respective retention practices and privacy policies.

Your privacy rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, or delete personal information, or to object to or restrict certain uses of it.

Because Whatcha doesn't require an account and stores learning progress locally, the information we may hold ourselves is generally limited to support correspondence and problem reports you've submitted.

You can contact us to make a privacy request. We aim to respond within 30 days, or sooner where required by applicable law.

For information processed independently by Google or Apple, you can also review their privacy policies and available privacy controls.

Security

We take reasonable steps to protect the information handled by Whatcha.

Learning data is stored locally and benefits from your iPhone's built-in security protections. Problem reports are transmitted to our reporting service over encrypted HTTPS connections.

No method of storing or transmitting information is completely secure, but we aim to minimize the information we collect and protect the information we do handle.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as Whatcha changes.

When we do, we'll update the effective date at the top of this page. If we make significant changes to how the app collects or uses information, we'll also explain them in the app's release notes or through another appropriate notice.
